Using scientific functions safely

A scientific calculator is useful for powers, roots, trigonometry and multi-step arithmetic. Use parentheses to make the intended order clear—for example, enter (2 + 3) × 4 when you want 20 rather than 2 + (3 × 4), which equals 14.

Angles and precision

Angle mode: This calculator currently evaluates sin, cos and tan in radians. For example, sin(1) means the sine of 1 radian, not 1 degree. Convert degrees to radians by multiplying by π ÷ 180 before entering the angle.

  • Check whether trigonometric functions expect degrees or radians before calculating sin, cos or tan.
  • Keep extra digits during intermediate steps and round only the final result.
  • Some expressions are undefined, such as division by zero; logarithms of non-positive real numbers also need special handling.
  • For exams or engineering work, verify required mode and rounding rules against the calculator policy or specification.

Results are numerical aids and should be independently checked when used for safety-critical or high-stakes work.

Scientific calculator guide

A scientific calculator extends basic arithmetic with functions such as powers, roots, logarithms and trigonometry. Enter expressions carefully and use parentheses to make the intended order explicit. For example, (2 + 3) × 4 equals 20, while 2 + 3 × 4 equals 14 because multiplication is evaluated before addition.

Angles, logarithms and domain limits

This calculator’s trigonometric functions use radians. Convert degrees to radians by multiplying degrees by π ÷ 180; therefore 90° equals π/2 radians. Logarithms require a positive input, and division by zero or operations outside a function’s mathematical domain may produce an invalid result. Displayed decimal values may be rounded, so keep more precision for intermediate steps when accuracy matters.

Frequently asked questions

Why is sin(90) not 1?

The calculator uses radians, so sin(90) means 90 radians. For 90 degrees, enter π/2 radians or convert degrees first.

Why is the logarithm result invalid?

Real-valued logarithms require a positive input.

Why does a result show rounded decimals?

The display limits decimal places for readability; the internal calculation may have more precision.

Can I use it for a graded exam?

Follow your exam rules and permitted calculator policy. This page does not determine whether a device or tool is allowed.
