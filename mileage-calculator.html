How to calculate mileage

Vehicle mileage is the distance travelled divided by the fuel consumed. Enter the kilometres driven and litres of petrol or diesel used to calculate your vehicle's fuel efficiency in km/l.

Example mileage calculation

If a car travels 300 km using 20 litres of fuel, its estimated mileage is 300 ÷ 20 = 15 km/l. For a more representative figure, calculate mileage over several trips rather than relying on one short journey.

Use mileage to estimate fuel cost

Knowing your km/l makes trip planning easier. Use your result with the Fuel Cost Calculator to estimate petrol or diesel expenses, or use the Trip Cost Calculator for a broader road-trip estimate.

Measure real-world mileage accurately

The basic formula is distance travelled in kilometres ÷ fuel consumed in litres = km/L. For example, if you travel 420 km and use 28 litres, your mileage is 420 ÷ 28 = 15 km/L.

Tank-to-tank method

  1. Fill the tank to the same pump's first automatic cut-off when practical; avoid repeatedly topping up.
  2. Reset the trip meter and drive normally.
  3. Refill using the same method and record the litres added and distance travelled.
  4. Divide distance by litres. Repeat over several fills to smooth out small measurement differences.

Short trips, traffic, tyre pressure, driving style, load, weather and fuel-pump variation can affect the result. A dashboard estimate may differ from a tank-to-tank calculation. To estimate fuel expenditure for a planned route, use the Fuel Cost Calculator.

Real-world mileage: what the result means

Mileage is commonly expressed as kilometres per litre (km/L) in India. The calculation is distance travelled divided by fuel consumed. If a vehicle travels 420 km using 28 litres, its measured mileage is 420 ÷ 28 = 15 km/L. Use distance and fuel units from the same trip, and avoid mixing odometer kilometres with fuel quantities in gallons.

Get a more reliable measurement

The tank-to-tank method is usually more useful than a single dashboard reading: fill the tank safely, reset the trip meter, drive normally, refill at a comparable pump and nozzle cut-off, then divide the kilometres travelled by the litres added. Repeat over several tanks to smooth out differences caused by traffic, weather, idling, tyre pressure and route. Never overfill a tank after the nozzle automatically stops.

Estimate fuel cost

Fuel used = distance ÷ mileage. Estimated fuel cost = fuel used × price per litre. For a 300 km journey at 15 km/L, estimated fuel use is 20 litres. At ₹105 per litre, that is about ₹2,100, excluding tolls, parking and maintenance. Actual results vary with load, speed, traffic and vehicle condition.

Frequently asked questions

Is higher km/L better?

Yes, for the same distance and comparable conditions, higher km/L means less fuel is used.

Why does my dashboard show a different number?

Dashboard estimates and tank-to-tank measurements use different methods and may vary with recent driving conditions.

Can I compare two vehicles directly?

Use the same route, load, driving style and measurement method where possible. Official test figures may not match everyday use.

Does this predict exact mileage?

No. It calculates mileage from the values entered; it cannot account for every road or vehicle condition.
