How to calculate concrete volume

For a rectangular slab, multiply length × width × depth using the same unit for all three measurements. If measurements are in metres, the result is cubic metres (m³). The optional wastage allowance is applied after the basic volume.

Worked example

A slab 5 m long, 4 m wide and 0.10 m thick needs 5 × 4 × 0.10 = 2.00 m³ of concrete. With a 5% allowance, the estimate is 2.00 × 1.05 = 2.10 m³.

Before ordering

  • Measure the actual dimensions and check whether the thickness varies.
  • Split irregular shapes into rectangular sections and add their volumes.
  • Confirm the appropriate mix, strength grade, reinforcement and site preparation with a qualified professional.
  • Ask the supplier how it rounds delivery quantities and whether minimum-load or delivery charges apply.

This tool estimates volume only. It does not design structural members, specify a concrete mix, or account for every site condition. Verify dimensions and engineering requirements before construction.

Concrete calculator FAQs

How much concrete do I need for a 5 m × 4 m slab?

At 0.10 m thickness, the base volume is 5 × 4 × 0.10 = 2.00 m³. With 5% wastage, the planning estimate is 2.10 m³.

Why add a wastage allowance?

Small differences in dimensions, uneven ground, spillage and handling can increase the amount required. The correct allowance depends on the job; this calculator lets you choose an allowance from 0% to 50%.

Can I use this for a footing or column?

It can estimate the volume of a rectangular section when the dimensions are known. Irregular or tapered shapes need to be split into suitable sections and calculated separately.

Does this calculate cement bags and sand?

No. It estimates finished concrete volume only. Material quantities depend on the specified mix design, aggregate moisture, batching method and supplier guidance.

Safety note: Confirm structural dimensions, concrete grade, reinforcement and site requirements with the responsible qualified professional before ordering or building.
