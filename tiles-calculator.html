How to calculate tiles for a room

First calculate the room area by multiplying its length by width. Convert the room and tile measurements to the same unit, then divide the room area by the area covered by one tile. The calculator rounds up to a whole tile because tiles cannot normally be purchased as fractions.

Allow for tile wastage

Cutting, breakage, edge pieces and installation patterns can create waste. A 10% allowance is a useful starting point for a straightforward project, while rooms with many corners, diagonal layouts or complex patterns may require a different allowance.

Example tile calculation

For a 20 ft × 15 ft room, the floor area is 300 sq ft. If each tile covers 2 ft × 2 ft, one tile covers 4 sq ft, so 75 tiles are needed before wastage. With 10% wastage, the recommended quantity is rounded up to 83 tiles.

Tile calculator for flooring and walls

This tool can help with basic flooring or wall-tile planning. Enter the actual dimensions of the project and tile package information where available. For a final purchase quantity, also consider manufacturer packaging, spare tiles and the installation method.

Plan tiles for rooms with more than one section

For an L-shaped room, split the floor into rectangles, calculate each rectangle's area, and add the areas before estimating tiles. For wall tiles, measure each wall separately and subtract doors or windows when you need the net tiled area. Keep floor and wall areas separate so they are not accidentally added together.

Wastage, cartons and purchase quantity

The wastage percentage is an estimate, not a guarantee. Straight layouts in a simple rectangular room may need less cutting than diagonal layouts, small rooms with many corners, patterned designs or fragile materials. The calculator rounds the tile count up to a whole tile, but suppliers may sell tiles in cartons; use the manufacturer's coverage per carton to round up again before ordering.

If the calculated requirement is 83 tiles, for example, and a carton contains 6 tiles, you would need 14 cartons (84 tiles). Keep a few spare tiles for future repairs where practical, and confirm the final quantity with the installer or supplier. Tile thickness, grout gaps, substrate preparation and the laying pattern are not included in this area estimate.

Check the assumptions before buying

  • Measure the actual room rather than relying only on a floor plan.
  • Use the same unit for both dimensions of each room and tile.
  • Enter the price per individual tile, not the price per carton, in the optional price field.
  • Compare the estimated coverage and quantity with the product packaging.

Worked example: a 12 ft × 10 ft room

A 12 ft by 10 ft room has an area of 120 sq ft. If each tile is 2 ft by 2 ft, one tile covers 4 sq ft, so the theoretical requirement is 120 ÷ 4 = 30 tiles. With a 10% allowance for cuts and breakage, 30 × 1.10 = 33 tiles. Round up to whole tiles and then check how many tiles come in each carton.

Why the estimate can differ from the actual order

  • Diagonal patterns and many corners usually create more offcuts than a straight layout.
  • Tiles may need to be cut around columns, doorways, drains or fixtures.
  • Tiles from different batches can vary slightly in shade or size; retain spare tiles for future repairs.
  • The calculator assumes a rectangular area and uniform tile size. It does not create a layout plan or account for grout-joint spacing.

Before you purchase

Measure the finished surface, confirm the exact tile dimensions printed on the carton, and use the manufacturer's coverage per box where available. If a room has alcoves or multiple sections, calculate each section separately and add the results. Treat the wastage percentage as a planning allowance rather than a guarantee.

Tile buying guide: area, cartons and wastage

Calculate the floor or wall area before estimating tile quantity. Divide the area to be covered by the coverage of one tile, then round up to a whole tile. If tiles are sold by carton, use the coverage printed on the carton and round up to whole cartons. Do not assume every carton contains the same number of tiles or covers the same area.

Worked example

A 12 ft × 10 ft room is 120 sq ft. With a 10% allowance, plan for 132 sq ft of tile coverage. If one carton covers 16 sq ft, 132 ÷ 16 = 8.25 cartons, so you would need 9 cartons, assuming the printed coverage is accurate and the layout suits the tiles. Keep spare tiles from the same batch for future repairs.

Frequently asked questions

Why add wastage?

Tiles may need cutting around edges, doors, columns and drains; some may break or be unsuitable. Patterned or diagonal layouts can need more spare material.

Should I round down the number of cartons?

No. Round up to a whole carton so the planned coverage is not short, then verify pack coverage and return policies with the seller.

Does this include skirting or wall tiles?

Only if those areas are measured and entered separately. Floor area does not automatically include skirting or walls.

Can I use the estimate as an exact order?

Treat it as a planning estimate. Confirm room measurements, tile dimensions, grout gaps, layout and carton coverage with the installer or supplier before ordering.
