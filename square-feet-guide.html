How to Calculate Square Feet for Construction

A practical area guide for rooms, floors, tiles, paint and building measurements.

Basic square-feet formula

Area (square feet) = Length (feet) × Width (feet)

If a room is 12 feet long and 10 feet wide, its area is 12 × 10 = 120 square feet.

When measurements are in inches

Convert inches to feet first, or calculate square inches and divide by 144.

Square feet = Length (in) × Width (in) ÷ 144

For example, 77 × 33 inches = 2,541 square inches, which is 17.646 square feet.

Using centimetres or metres

For centimetres, convert each measurement to feet before multiplying, or convert the final square-centimetre result to square feet. For metres, one square metre is approximately 10.7639 square feet.

Rooms with multiple sections

Split an L-shaped or irregular room into rectangles, calculate each rectangle separately, then add the areas. Keep the same unit throughout the calculation to avoid conversion errors.

Why accurate area matters

Area is often the starting point for estimating tiles, paint, flooring and other materials. Material purchases may need extra allowance for cuts, breakage, overlaps or installation waste, depending on the project.

Worked examples you can check

Room measured in feet

A room measuring 12 ft by 10 ft has an area of 12 × 10 = 120 sq ft. If a second rectangular section measures 4 ft by 3 ft, add 12 sq ft to get 132 sq ft total.

Measurements in feet and inches

Convert inches to decimal feet before multiplying. For example, 10 ft 6 in is 10.5 ft. A 10 ft 6 in by 8 ft room is 10.5 × 8 = 84 sq ft.

Square metres to square feet

Multiply square metres by 10.7639. A 20 m² floor is approximately 215.28 sq ft. Keep unrounded values during the calculation and round only the final result.

Estimating material quantities

Area is not always the same as the quantity to buy. Tiles need allowances for cuts and breakage; paint coverage depends on the product, surface texture and number of coats. Measure doorways, niches and irregular areas carefully, and follow the supplier's coverage guidance. For expensive work, verify measurements on site before ordering.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Multiplying measurements in different units without converting them first.
  • Adding lengths instead of calculating each rectangular area's length × width.
  • Forgetting to split L-shaped rooms into separate rectangles.
  • Rounding each small section too early, which can create a larger total error.
