How to estimate EV charging cost

A simple estimate is energy added in kWh multiplied by the electricity price per kWh. If charging from 20% to 80% on a 60 kWh battery, the nominal energy added is 60 × (0.80 − 0.20) = 36 kWh. At ₹8 per kWh, that is ₹288 before charging losses or extra fees.

Actual wall energy can be higher because charging is not perfectly efficient. Battery capacity shown by a vehicle may be usable capacity rather than the advertised gross capacity; public chargers can also have session, parking or time-based fees. Use your tariff and charger bill for the closest estimate.

This is a cost estimate, not a prediction of driving range. See the EV range calculator for a separate range estimate and the trip cost calculator for road-trip planning.

Estimate EV charging cost

For a simple home-charging estimate, multiply the energy drawn from the grid in kilowatt-hours by your electricity tariff per kWh. The energy drawn from the grid can be higher than the energy stored in the battery because charging is not perfectly efficient. Check your bill for the relevant tariff and any time-of-use rates, fixed charges or public-charger fees that may apply.

Worked example

If you add 30 kWh from the grid at ₹8 per kWh, the energy charge is approximately 30 × ₹8 = ₹240. If you start with a battery percentage and target percentage, the energy added can be estimated from usable battery capacity and the percentage-point difference; the final cost still depends on charging losses and the tariff.

Frequently asked questions

Does this include charging losses?

Only if your input energy estimate accounts for them. Grid energy may exceed the energy stored in the battery.

Is home charging always cheaper?

Not necessarily. Compare your actual home tariff with the public charging operator’s rates, parking fees and session fees.

Why does my bill differ from the estimate?

Tariff slabs, taxes, time-of-use rates, charger efficiency and battery temperature can affect the total.

Does this show live charger prices?

No. Enter a current tariff from your bill or charging provider.
