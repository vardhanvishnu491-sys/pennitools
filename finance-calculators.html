Worked example: understand a loan EMI

Suppose you borrow ₹1,00,000 for 12 months at a nominal annual interest rate of 12%, with monthly reducing-balance interest and no fees. The monthly rate is 1% and the estimated EMI is about ₹8,885; total repayments are about ₹1,06,620, so interest is about ₹6,620. Your lender may calculate differently because of processing fees, dates, insurance or loan-specific terms. Change the amount, rate and tenure in the EMI Calculator to explore the effect.

How to check a GST-inclusive price

If a product costs ₹1,180 including 18% GST, the pre-tax value is ₹1,000 and the GST portion is ₹180. For an inclusive price, divide by 1 + the tax rate expressed as a decimal; do not simply subtract 18% of the inclusive price. Verify the applicable rate and invoice treatment for the actual product or service.

CTC to in-hand salary: how the estimate works

Cost to Company (CTC) is the annual cost an employer budgets for an employee. It is not the same as monthly take-home pay. CTC may include fixed salary, employer provident fund contributions, gratuity, insurance, variable bonus and other benefits. Monthly in-hand salary is the amount paid after employee-side deductions and any applicable tax withholding. The exact salary structure differs by employer and tax choices, so use your offer letter or payslip for a more accurate breakdown.

What can be deducted?

Common items include employee provident fund (EPF), professional tax where applicable, income-tax TDS, insurance premiums or other authorised payroll deductions. Some CTC components are annual or conditional and may not appear in every monthly payslip. Variable pay or a performance bonus may also be paid only when eligibility conditions are met. This simple calculator estimates take-home from the percentages you enter; it does not determine your legal tax liability or reproduce every company salary structure.

Worked example

Suppose annual CTC is ₹6,00,000 and you use illustrative deductions of 12% for employee PF and 8% for other estimated deductions. The calculator’s simplified estimate is ₹6,00,000 × (1 − 0.12 − 0.08) ÷ 12 = ₹50,000 per month. This is only an example: actual PF may be based on eligible salary components, tax depends on applicable rules and declarations, and employer contributions included in CTC may be treated differently from employee deductions.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my in-hand salary lower than CTC divided by 12?

CTC can include employer contributions, benefits and variable amounts as well as cash salary. Payroll deductions also reduce the amount paid to you.

Is this estimate an official tax calculation?

No. It is a simplified estimate based on inputs. Use current official tax guidance and your payroll team for a personal calculation.

Does every employee pay the same PF percentage?

No. PF treatment depends on eligible wages, applicable rules and the employer’s salary structure.

Can I rely on this number before accepting an offer?

Use it for an initial comparison only. Ask the employer for a salary breakup showing fixed pay, variable pay, employer contributions and expected monthly deductions.

Frequently asked questions

Is the EMI shown by this site the exact bank EMI?

It is a mathematical estimate based on the principal, annual interest rate and repayment period entered. A lender may calculate fees, insurance, daily interest, rate changes or rounding differently. Compare the result with the lender’s official repayment schedule.

Does a lower EMI always mean a cheaper loan?

No. A longer tenure often reduces the monthly instalment but can increase total interest. Compare both the monthly payment and the total amount repaid, including fees.

How do I calculate GST from a price that already includes tax?

Divide the tax-inclusive price by 1 plus the GST rate as a decimal to get the pre-tax value. For example, ₹1,180 at 18% GST corresponds to ₹1,000 before tax and ₹180 GST, subject to the correct rate and invoice treatment.

Can I use these results for filing taxes or choosing a loan?

Use the figures for planning and checking arithmetic, not as a final tax determination or financial recommendation. Verify current rules and product terms with official sources or a qualified professional.
